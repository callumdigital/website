The problem

Students at Victoria University use a long list of digital services, from enrolment through to graduation. Every touchpoint is a chance to support them or frustrate them.

The challenge was working out where the pain points actually were, and how to make the whole thing feel like one experience instead of many.

What we found

Interviews with students across different years and study areas, journey mapping workshops with students and staff, and a look through support requests.

  1. Transitions hurt most. Starting uni, changing courses and getting ready to graduate all cross multiple systems with unclear steps.
  2. Students don't need more services. They need clearer paths through the ones that exist. Info was spread across systems with no obvious place to start.
  3. Needs change by stage. First-years want orientation and the basics. Final-years want career prep and help with what's next. One size fits nobody.

What I did

Turned the research into four principles for every digital service:

  • Meet students where they already are
  • Show info that fits their stage and situation
  • Make processes visible and progress trackable
  • Design for the moments that matter most

Result

The map and research surfaced quick wins and longer-term strategic work. The focus now is on the critical transitions, so the digital experience grows with students through their time at uni.

In a big organisation, bringing stakeholders along is half the job. Service design isn't just about the end users. It's about helping everyone see the whole system and their part in fixing it.
